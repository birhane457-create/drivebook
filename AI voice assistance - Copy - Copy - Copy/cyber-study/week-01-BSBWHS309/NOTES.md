# Week 1 — BSBWHS309: Contribute to WHS Communication and Consultation

## What this unit is actually about

WHS = Work Health and Safety. In an IT/cyber context this covers:
- Ergonomic risks (sitting at a desk 8+ hrs)
- Psychological safety (managing stress, burnout, incident pressure)
- Reporting hazards in a workplace
- How WHS consultation processes work (who gets consulted, when, how)

For a solo founder this is mostly about **documentation** — proving you understand the process and could implement it in a team.

---

## Key Concepts

### 1. WHS Legislation (WA)
- **Work Health and Safety Act 2020 (WA)** — came into force March 2022
- Replaced the old OSH Act 1984
- Applies to all workers including contractors and volunteers
- Key duty: employers must ensure work is safe "so far as is reasonably practicable"

### 2. Hazard Identification
A hazard is anything with potential to cause harm.

In an IT/cyber role:
| Hazard type | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| Physical | Repetitive strain from keyboard use |
| Psychological | On-call stress, incident response pressure, isolation (remote work) |
| Environmental | Poor lighting, uncomfortable chair |
| Cyber | Working with sensitive customer data — breach stress |

### 3. WHS Consultation Process
Must involve workers in decisions that affect their health and safety.
- **HSR** = Health and Safety Representative (elected by workers)
- **WHS Committee** = joint management/worker body
- Consultation ≠ just being told — workers must be able to influence decisions

### 4. Hazard Reporting
- Hazard → Report → Assess → Control → Review
- **Hierarchy of Controls** (most to least effective):
  1. Eliminate
  2. Substitute
  3. Isolate
  4. Engineering controls
  5. Administrative controls
  6. PPE (Personal Protective Equipment)

## Practice Question

**Q:** You are the IT manager for a small company. You're about to deploy new monitoring software on all staff computers. What WHS consultation process should you follow before deployment?

**Model answer:**
1. Identify affected workers (all staff whose computers are being monitored)
2. Provide written notice of the proposed change and its purpose
3. Hold a team meeting where staff can raise concerns (privacy, surveillance anxiety)
4. Document all concerns raised
5. Assess whether monitoring creates psychological harm (feeling of distrust)
6. Apply hierarchy of controls — if monitoring is necessary, use administrative controls (clear policy on what is/isn't monitored, communicated to all staff)
7. Obtain sign-off from HSR if one exists
8. Proceed with deployment only after consultation is complete
