Most driving instructors know that reviews help them look trustworthy. Fewer realise that reviews are also one of the most direct factors in how Google decides which instructor to show first in local search results.

## How Google Uses Reviews for Local Ranking

Google's local search algorithm weighs three broad factors: relevance, distance, and prominence.

Reviews are a key component of prominence — how well-known and well-regarded a business is in Google's eyes.

Specifically, Google looks at:

**Review quantity** — More reviews signal that more people have used and rated your service. An instructor with 40 reviews ranks higher than one with 4, assuming other factors are similar.

**Review recency** — Recent reviews signal an active business. A profile with reviews that are all two years old looks less current than one receiving reviews regularly.

**Review content** — Google reads the text of reviews, not just the star rating. Reviews that mention suburbs ("great instructor in Cannington"), specific skills ("helped me with parallel parking"), and outcomes ("passed first time") give Google additional signals about your service area and expertise.

**Review responses** — Responding to reviews, especially negative ones, signals engagement and professionalism. Google notices and rewards this.

## The Practical Gap

Most driving instructors have significantly fewer reviews than they could. A busy instructor who teaches 25 students per week and has been operating for three years has had potentially 3,000+ students. If they have 20 Google reviews, they've captured less than 1% of possible testimonials.

The gap isn't because students are unwilling to leave reviews. It's because they weren't asked, or asking felt awkward, or the process wasn't easy enough.

## How to Close the Gap

**Ask immediately after the test** — The best moment to ask for a review is right after a student passes their PDA. Emotions are high, the experience is fresh, and the student is genuinely grateful. A brief, direct ask — "Would you mind leaving a Google review? It really helps" — with a link works very well at this moment.

**Make it one tap** — Send a direct link to your Google review page via SMS. Students who have to search for your business to leave a review often don't. A link removes all friction.

**Ask on DriveBook too** — Your DriveBook profile review count is visible to students comparing instructors. Both platforms contribute to your overall credibility.

**Don't ask for five stars** — Asking for a "five-star review" violates Google's terms and can result in reviews being removed. Ask for "a review" — genuine reviews naturally tend to reflect genuine experiences.

## What Good Reviews Look Like

A review that says "great instructor, highly recommend" is fine. A review that says "Patient with my nerves on roundabouts, knew all the test routes around Joondalup, and I passed on my first attempt after 12 lessons" is far more valuable — both for conversion and for SEO.

You can't write students' reviews for them, but you can ask specific questions that prompt more useful responses: "What would you tell a friend looking for an instructor in [suburb]?"

## The Compound Effect

Reviews compound. An instructor with 50 genuine reviews is much harder to displace from the top of local search results than one with 10. And the more visible they are, the more students they attract, which generates more reviews.

Starting early — asking consistently from your first students — builds this advantage faster than waiting until you're established.
