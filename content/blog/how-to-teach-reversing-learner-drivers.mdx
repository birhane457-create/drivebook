Reversing is one of the skills that separates confident drivers from cautious ones. For learner drivers, it's often the area that needs the most deliberate practice — and it's one of the most common sources of faults in the Practical Driving Assessment.

The good news: reversing is a skill, and skills can be learned. With the right approach and enough practice, it becomes instinctive.

## Why Reversing Feels Hard

When you drive forward, you look where you're going, and your hands and feet respond naturally. Reversing reverses that logic in a way the brain finds less intuitive.

When you steer left while reversing, the back of the car goes left — but because you're facing the other direction, this can feel like the car is going the wrong way. Add in the limited rear visibility most vehicles have, and it's easy to understand why learners find it difficult.

The solution is practice until the steering response feels natural, not continuing to think through it each time.

## Reversing Straight

Before you practise any parking manoeuvre, practise reversing in a straight line. Find a quiet car park or road, and reverse as straight as possible over a distance of about 30–40 metres.

Key points:
- Look through the rear window, not the mirrors
- Use small steering corrections — don't overcorrect
- Move slowly. Speed makes all reversing harder.
- Check mirrors regularly during the reverse

Practice this until you can reverse straight consistently. It's the foundation of everything else.

## Reverse Parallel Parking

Parallel parking — reversing into a space alongside the kerb — is assessed in the PDA and required in everyday driving. There are different methods, but a reliable sequence for beginners:

**Step 1:** Pull alongside the car in front of the target space. Position your car roughly parallel to it, about half a metre to a side, with your front bumper aligned approximately with the other car's rear bumper.

**Step 2:** Check mirrors and blind spots. Select reverse.

**Step 3:** Begin reversing slowly. When your rear axle is approximately level with the rear of the car beside you, steer hard to the left (assuming you're parking on the left side of the road).

**Step 4:** When your car is at roughly 45 degrees to the kerb, steer right to straighten up as you continue reversing toward the space.

**Step 5:** When parallel to the kerb and within 30cm, stop. Apply the handbrake.

**Common mistakes:**
- Starting too far from the reference car (makes the angle too shallow)
- Not steering sharply enough on the first move
- Moving too fast to correct in time

This will feel mechanical at first. With practice it becomes intuitive. Aim to practise on different sized spaces and different length vehicles to develop flexibility.

## Reversing Around a Corner

Reversing around a corner is sometimes included in PDA assessments and requires coordinating observation with smooth steering.

The key technique:
- Look through the rear window first, not at the mirrors
- When the curve of the corner appears in your rear window, steer to follow it
- Check mirrors and side windows regularly to monitor your distance from the kerb
- Keep speed very low — walking pace

The most common error is over-steering and mounting the kerb. Move slowly enough to make corrections before it's too late.

## Observation While Reversing

Before you reverse, always check:
- Rear window (directly through the glass)
- Driver's side mirror
- Passenger's side mirror
- Blind spots over both shoulders

Continue checking regularly during the manoeuvre. Your assessor will fail you for insufficient observation even if the physical execution is perfect.

## How Much Practice Is Enough?

There's no magic number, but most learners need significant dedicated practice specifically on reversing — not just occasional reverses as part of other lessons. Ask your instructor to dedicate full lessons to parking and reversing manoeuvres rather than including them briefly at the end.

If you're doing 50 hours of supervised driving, ensure a meaningful portion of that covers reversing in various situations.
