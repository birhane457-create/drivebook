When you complete your Driving Instructor Authorisation and start thinking about building a client base, the first decision is often: do I join an established driving school, or do I work independently?

Both have genuine advantages. The right answer depends on where you are in your career and what you want from it.

## The Driving School Path

### Advantages

**Established client pipeline.** The biggest advantage: a school with a good local reputation already has students booking through them. As a new instructor, you inherit an inquiry stream rather than building one from scratch.

**Administrative support.** Scheduling, payment processing, and sometimes even vehicle maintenance may be handled by the school, letting you focus on teaching.

**Brand credibility.** Students booking through an established school feel some assurance about quality. As an independent instructor just starting out, building that trust takes time.

**Vehicle access.** Some schools provide vehicles, reducing your startup capital requirements.

### Disadvantages

**Revenue split.** The school takes a cut of your earnings — often 20–40% depending on the arrangement. This significantly affects your income ceiling.

**Limited control.** You may not control your schedule, your pricing, or how you interact with students. Some schools are very prescriptive about teaching methodology.

**Dependency.** If the school loses clients or changes management, your income is affected by decisions outside your control.

**Exit restrictions.** Some agreements include non-compete clauses that restrict you from taking clients independently when you leave.

## The Independent Path

### Advantages

**You keep everything you earn.** Platform fees aside, you don't share revenue with anyone. A full schedule as an independent instructor earns significantly more than the same schedule under a school split.

**Full control.** Your pricing, your schedule, your methodology, your brand.

**Direct relationship with students.** Your reputation is yours — not shared with a school name. Students who value you stay with you.

**Scalability.** If you eventually want to build a team or expand, you can do that under your own brand.

### Disadvantages

**You have to find your own students.** This is the real challenge in the first 6-12 months. Without a pipeline, you need to build one — through reviews, referrals, and your online presence.

**All admin falls on you.** Booking, payments, reminders, scheduling — unless you use a tool that automates these.

**No vehicle support.** You own and maintain your own vehicle.

## The Middle Path: Independent with Platform Support

The independent path has become significantly more viable in the last few years because tools now handle most of what schools used to provide — minus the revenue split.

- A public profile that students can find when searching for instructors in their area
- Online booking that replaces phone/SMS scheduling
- Automated reminders that reduce no-shows
- Prepaid package support so students commit financially upfront
- Lesson feedback and student progress tracking to build the retention schools provide

The difference: you keep full control of your pricing, your schedule, and your earnings. A school keeps 20–40%. A platform like DriveBook charges a flat subscription — the economics are very different.

## Which to Choose

**If you're brand new:** Starting with an established school while you build skills and confidence isn't wrong. The client pipeline reduces the risk of your first year. But read any agreement carefully before you sign — especially around exit terms.

**If you've been instructing for 1+ years:** The independent path almost always makes more financial sense once you have reviews and a reputation to build on. The revenue difference over a full schedule is too significant to ignore.
