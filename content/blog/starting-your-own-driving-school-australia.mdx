Starting a driving school in Australia is a realistic goal for an experienced driving instructor, but it requires more than teaching ability. It's a business — and like any business, it has regulatory, financial, and operational requirements that need to be in place before you start.

This article gives you a practical overview. Requirements vary by state, so always verify current rules with your relevant transport authority.

## Step 1: Get the Right Authorisation

In Western Australia, driving instructors must hold a Driving Instructor Authorisation issued by the Department of Transport. This requires meeting eligibility criteria including a clean driving record, criminal history check, and passing the required assessments. Check the current requirements at the DoT WA website — these can change.

If you're already authorised as an individual instructor, operating as a driving school (with employed or contracted instructors) may have additional licensing requirements. Confirm with DoT WA what applies to your specific structure.

## Step 2: Choose Your Business Structure

The most common structures for a driving school:

**Sole trader** — Simplest and cheapest to set up. You operate under your own name (or a registered business name). All income is yours; all liability is yours. Suits solo instructors.

**Company (Pty Ltd)** — More complex to set up, but provides limited liability protection. Better suited once you're employing instructors or carrying significant equipment assets. Requires annual ASIC filing.

**Partnership** — Two or more people operating together. Useful if you're starting with a co-founder. Requires a partnership agreement.

Most new driving schools start as sole traders and convert to a company structure as they grow.

## Step 3: Register Your Business

Register your business name with ASIC if you're not trading under your personal name. Apply for an ABN through the ATO — you'll need this for invoicing, supplier agreements, and to register a `.com.au` domain.

If your annual turnover is expected to exceed the GST threshold (check the ATO website for the current figure), you'll need to register for GST and lodge regular BAS statements.

## Step 4: Insurance

At minimum you need:

**Comprehensive car insurance** — Standard personal car insurance typically does not cover commercial driving instruction use. You need a policy that explicitly covers your use as a driving instructor. Get this right before you take a single student.

**Public liability insurance** — Covers claims arising from your instruction activities. Required by most industry associations and many driving school insurance packages include it.

**Professional indemnity** — Covers claims related to your professional advice or instruction. Increasingly standard for instructors.

Get quotes from insurers who specifically cover driving instructors — rates and inclusions vary significantly.

## Step 5: Your Vehicle

Your vehicle needs to be:
- Dual-controlled (instructor-accessible brake as minimum requirement)
- Maintained to a roadworthy standard at all times
- Insured for commercial driving instruction use (see above)

If you're building a school with multiple instructors, each instructor needs their own compliant vehicle. Leasing or financing vehicles for instructors is a common approach for growing schools.

## Step 6: Set Up Your Operations

**Booking system** — A platform like DriveBook handles online booking, payment collection, student records, calendar management, and automated reminders. Starting with the right system prevents the pain of migrating from manual systems later.

**Bank account** — A dedicated business bank account keeps your business income separate from personal finances, which simplifies tax and bookkeeping.

**Accountant** — Find an accountant familiar with small service businesses early. They'll help you set up correctly from the start rather than fixing problems retrospectively.

## Step 7: Your First Instructors

If you're expanding beyond solo operation, you need to decide whether additional instructors will be:

**Employees** — You pay their super, withhold PAYG tax, and take on employment law obligations. More responsibility, but more control.

**Contractors** — They invoice you, handle their own tax and super. Less administrative burden, but the ATO has specific tests for whether a contractor relationship is genuine. Get advice before assuming contractors aren't employees.

## The Reality of Running a School

The jump from solo instructor to school owner is significant. You're no longer just teaching — you're managing people, handling finances, dealing with customer complaints that aren't about your own lessons, and maintaining a brand that multiple instructors are operating under.

The reward is a business that runs beyond your own available teaching hours. A well-run five-instructor school earns income across five schedules, not just yours.
