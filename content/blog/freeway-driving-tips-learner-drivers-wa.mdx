Driving on the freeway for the first time is one of the moments many learner drivers dread. High speeds, merging traffic, and multiple lanes can feel overwhelming when you've been practising in quiet streets.

The good news: freeway driving is actually less complicated than it looks. There are fewer pedestrians, no traffic lights, and everyone is moving in the same direction. Once you understand the rules and develop a feel for the speed, most learners find freeways significantly less stressful than city driving.

## When Do Learner Drivers in WA Need to Drive on the Freeway?

Your logbook hours must include a range of driving conditions to meet Western Australia's requirements. Freeway driving counts toward your 50 hours and is expected before your PDA.

Your assessor may include freeway driving in your Practical Driving Assessment, depending on the test centre location. Freeway routes are common from test centres in metropolitan Perth, including Midland, Joondalup, and Belmont.

Even if your PDA route doesn't include a freeway, you'll need freeway experience before you get your full licence.

## Speed and Following Distance

The speed limit on most Perth freeways is 100 km/h. This is higher than anything you've driven in suburban streets, and it takes time to get comfortable.

The key adjustment is following distance. At 100 km/h, stopping distance is significantly longer than at 60 km/h. Use the three-second rule as a minimum: pick a fixed point ahead, and when the car in front passes it, count three seconds before you reach the same point. In wet weather, extend this to four or five seconds.

A common mistake for new freeway drivers is following too closely — often because they're concentrating on the vehicle in front rather than scanning further ahead. Train yourself to look 10–15 seconds ahead of your position, not just at the car directly in front.

## Merging Onto the Freeway

Merging is the part most learners find hardest. The principle is straightforward: match your speed to traffic on the freeway before you enter.

On the on-ramp, accelerate smoothly so you're at or near freeway speed by the time you reach the merge point. Check your mirrors and blind spot for a gap in traffic, and merge smoothly. Don't stop at the end of an on-ramp unless it's completely unavoidable — this creates a dangerous situation.

Signal early, look for a gap, and commit. Hesitation causes more problems than a confident, smooth merge.

## Lane Discipline

In WA, keep left unless overtaking. This applies on freeways and is a common test failure point. The right lane is for overtaking — not for cruising.

If you're travelling at or below the speed limit, you should be in the left lane. Move right to overtake, complete the overtake, and return left.

When changing lanes:
1. Check your mirror (rear-view and side mirror for the lane you're moving into)
2. Check your blind spot over your shoulder
3. Signal
4. Move smoothly when clear

Lane changes on freeways should be gradual, not sharp. Sudden movements at high speed are dangerous.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

If you need to stop on the freeway — vehicle trouble, a tyre blow-out, or a medical issue — pull as far left as possible onto the hard shoulder or emergency stopping lane. Turn on your hazard lights immediately. Stay in the vehicle unless it's unsafe to do so, and call for assistance.

Never stop in a live lane.

## Practising Before Your PDA

Don't leave freeway practice until the week before your PDA. Build it in gradually:

- **First few sessions:** Shorter freeway sections at quieter times (early morning)
- **Mid-practice:** Longer sections including lane changes and multiple merges
- **Pre-PDA:** Practise the specific freeway sections near your test centre

Your driving instructor should know which routes the local test centre uses and can prepare you for exactly what you'll face.

## How to Find a Good Instructor for Freeway Practice

A good driving instructor will take you onto the freeway gradually, explain what to expect before you get there, and give you clear feedback afterwards. They shouldn't rush you onto a freeway before you're ready, but they also shouldn't delay it so long that you arrive at your PDA without experience.

When searching for an instructor, ask specifically about freeway experience in their lessons.
