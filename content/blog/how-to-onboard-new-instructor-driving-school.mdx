Adding a new instructor to your driving school is a growth milestone — but only if the onboarding is handled properly. A poorly onboarded instructor creates confusion, student experience problems, and in some cases, liability risk. A well-onboarded instructor is up and running quickly and represents the school well from day one.

## Before They Start: The Paperwork and Checks

Before a new instructor takes their first student under your school's name, certain verification steps are non-negotiable:

**Licence and certification:** Confirm their driving instructor licence is current and issued by the relevant state authority. In WA, this means verification through the Department of Transport. Never assume — check the document yourself.

**Working with Children check (WWC):** If they'll be teaching anyone under 18, a current WWC check is required in WA. This is a school owner's responsibility to verify.

**Insurance:** Confirm they have appropriate professional indemnity and public liability insurance, or are covered under your school's policy. Clarify this in writing before they teach a single student.

**ABN and contractor arrangement:** If your instructors work as independent contractors (common in the industry), ensure the arrangement is properly documented. Misclassifying an employee as a contractor has tax and legal implications. If you're uncertain, get advice from an accountant or employment lawyer.

## Introducing Them to Your Systems

Once the administrative foundations are in place, give the new instructor a proper systems walkthrough rather than throwing them in and hoping for the best.

On DriveBook, adding a new instructor to your school account gives them access to their own calendar, profile, and student management tools — within the visibility and settings you control as the school admin. Walk them through:

- Setting up their availability and working hours
- How their booking page appears to students
- The student wallet and payment flow (how they get paid)
- Lesson notes and progress tracking
- How you monitor performance from the admin dashboard

A new instructor who understands the system from day one makes fewer avoidable errors.

## Setting Expectations Upfront

Every school runs differently, and a new instructor coming from a different background or another school will arrive with assumptions that may not match yours. Cover these explicitly:

**Cancellation policy:** What is your school's policy? How is it communicated to students? Who handles disputes?

**Lesson documentation:** Do you require lesson notes after every session? What level of detail?

**Communication standards:** How should instructors communicate with students? Response time expectations, tone, booking-related questions.

**Schedule and availability:** What minimum availability do you expect? Are there hours they need to cover that aren't optional?

**Reporting:** How and when do you expect performance updates? What do you want to know about if problems arise?

Write these down. A short onboarding document that new instructors read and sign is worth the 30 minutes it takes to create.

## A Trial Period

Most school owners benefit from a structured trial period for new instructors — typically 30 to 60 days. During this period:

- Assign a manageable initial student load rather than filling their calendar immediately
- Check in weekly — brief, direct conversations about how it's going
- Review their lesson note quality and booking completion rate
- Get informal feedback from one or two early students if possible

Issues that emerge during a trial are easier to address (and terminate if needed) than issues that emerge after six months of full operation.

## Common Onboarding Mistakes

**Not doing the documentation checks:** Assuming a new instructor has the right credentials because they presented confidently. Always verify.

**Overwhelming them with students immediately:** A new instructor needs a ramp-up period. Starting them at 30 lessons per week before they understand your systems and students produces a poor first impression.

**No school induction:** Assuming they'll pick up your way of doing things by osmosis. They won't. Explicit onboarding documentation is faster and more reliable.

**Unclear payment terms:** Late or confusing payout information is one of the most reliable ways to sour a new employment or contractor relationship quickly. Be clear from day one: when they're paid, how much, under what calculation.

## Integrating Them Into the Team

If you have other instructors, introduce the new person properly rather than just adding them to a group chat. A brief in-person or video call introduction, and a clear message to your team about who they are and what they'll be covering, avoids awkwardness.

Culture matters even in small schools. An instructor who feels part of a team is more invested in its success than one who feels like a hired resource.
