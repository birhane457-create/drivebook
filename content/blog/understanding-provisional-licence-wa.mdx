Passing your PDA is a major milestone — but it's not the end of your supervised driving journey, it's the start of independent driving with restrictions. Western Australia's provisional licence system has two stages, each with its own conditions. Understanding them from day one keeps you on the right side of the rules.

## The Two Stages: P1 and P2

Western Australia uses a graduated licence system:

**P1 (Provisional 1):** Minimum 6 months. Red P plates.  
**P2 (Provisional 2):** Minimum 24 months. Green P plates.  

After P2, you progress to a full licence — provided you meet the requirements and haven't had conditions extended due to offences.

## P1 Conditions

On your P1 licence, the following conditions apply:

**Zero blood alcohol content (BAC).** You cannot drive with any alcohol in your system. The standard legal BAC for full licence holders is 0.05. For P1 drivers, it's zero.

**Speed restriction.** P1 licence holders cannot drive over 100 km/h, even where a higher limit is posted.

**No mobile phone use while driving.** This applies to all provisional drivers. No hands-free, no speaker. Phone must be off or in a cradle if used for navigation (and you must not touch it while moving).

**Restricted passengers.** Between 11pm and 5am, P1 drivers aged under 25 may carry only one passenger under 21 (unless a full-licence holder is also in the vehicle, or the passengers are immediate family members).

**No towing.** P1 drivers cannot tow a trailer or caravan.

**No driving with a BAC over zero.** As above — zero tolerance.

## P2 Conditions

After at least 6 months on P1 (and passing a hazard perception test if required), you progress to P2. The conditions relax somewhat:

**Zero BAC** — still applies.

**Speed restriction** — lifted on P2 (you can drive at the posted limit, including 110 km/h freeways).

**Passenger restrictions** — the nighttime passenger restriction is lifted on P2.

**Towing** — permitted with appropriate training and vehicle setup, subject to vehicle and licence conditions.

The main ongoing restrictions on P2 are zero BAC and the mobile phone rules. Both are enforced and both carry significant penalties.

## Demerit Points on P Plates

Demerit points work differently for provisional drivers than for full licence holders. The threshold is lower — accumulating a smaller number of points results in licence suspension.

P1 drivers have a 4-point threshold before suspension. For comparison, full licence holders have a 12-point threshold in a 3-year period.

Common offences that carry demerit points:
- Speeding (points increase with excess speed)
- Running a red light
- Failing to give way
- Mobile phone use while driving
- Not wearing a seatbelt

Take demerit point accumulation seriously on a P1. Four points goes quickly — a single speeding offence can cost two or three points alone.

## Progressing From P2 to Full Licence

After a minimum of 24 months on P2 with no licence suspensions or required extensions, you can apply for a full licence. This is typically straightforward — there's no additional test required, just a licence application through the DoT.

If you've had demerit point issues that extended your P2 period, or if you received a court-imposed disqualification, the timeline extends.

## Common Mistakes New P-Platers Make

**Forgetting the BAC is zero, not 0.05.** One drink is too many on a provisional licence. The standard 0.05 rule that applies to full licence holders doesn't apply to you.

**Exceeding 100 km/h on P1.** Some P1 drivers aren't aware the speed restriction applies even on roads with higher posted limits.

**Mobile phone in hand.** Any touching of the phone while driving is an offence — even at a red light.

**Not displaying P plates.** Your provisional plates must be clearly visible front and rear. Faded or obscured plates can result in a fine.

## The Point of the Graduated System

The restrictions exist because new drivers — even competent ones — are statistically at higher crash risk in the early years. Night driving, high speed, and distractions are the conditions where new driver crashes concentrate. The restrictions directly target these risk factors.

Think of it as the final phase of your driving development, not just a set of bureaucratic hoops. The habits you build in your first two or three years of independent driving shape how you drive for the rest of your life.
