Cancellations are expensive. A cancelled lesson is lost income, a wasted drive to the pickup address, and a gap in your schedule that's often too short-notice to fill.

Instructors who sell prepaid lesson packages report noticeably lower cancellation rates. The reason is simple psychology — and it's backed by how people behave with any prepaid commitment.

## The Sunk Cost Effect

When a student has already paid for a lesson, cancelling it feels like losing money. Even if the lesson can theoretically be rescheduled, the act of cancelling carries a psychological weight that prevents many students from doing so casually.

A student on a pay-per-lesson arrangement has no money at stake until the lesson happens. Cancelling feels free — there's nothing to lose. They can just book again later.

A student who has prepaid for ten lessons has already committed financially. Every lesson they don't attend is money they've paid for and aren't using. This alone shifts the incentive structure significantly.

## Higher Commitment From the Start

The act of buying a package signals intent. A student who purchases a ten-lesson package has made a deliberate decision to progress their learning. They're not casual enquirers — they've invested in a plan.

This self-selection matters. Package buyers are, on average, more motivated students who show up more consistently and progress faster. That's better for the student's outcome and better for the instructor's schedule predictability.

## How Packages Change Scheduling Behaviour

Students on packages tend to book their next lesson immediately after completing one, rather than waiting until they feel ready or have free time. The remaining balance is visible in their account. Each unscheduled lesson feels like a resource sitting unused.

This creates a virtuous cycle: regular lessons → faster progress → more confident student → better reviews → more referrals.

## Structured Options Work Better Than Open-Ended

The most effective packages have a defined lesson count. "Six lessons" or "ten lessons" is concrete and easy to reason about. "Save 10% on any future lessons" is vague and doesn't create the same commitment effect.

Giving the package a name and framing it around an outcome helps too. "PDA Preparation Pack — 8 lessons covering all test competencies" is more compelling than "8 lessons."

## What to Do About Genuine Cancellations

Even with packages, genuine cancellations happen. A clear, fair cancellation policy applied consistently is important:

- Cancellations with 24+ hours notice: lesson returned to package balance
- Cancellations under 24 hours: lesson forfeit (or partial credit at your discretion)
- Instructor-initiated cancellations: lesson always returned to balance

Communicate this at the time of purchase. Students who understand the policy before buying won't be surprised when it's applied.
