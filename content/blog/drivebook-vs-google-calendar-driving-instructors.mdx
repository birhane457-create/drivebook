Google Calendar is free, familiar, and works well for personal scheduling. Many driving instructors use it as their primary booking tool. Here's an honest look at what it does and doesn't do for a driving school operation.

## What Google Calendar Does Well

**Availability view** — A clear visual of your week, with lessons colour-coded and easy to scan. For solo instructors with a simple schedule, it's perfectly adequate.

**Sharing** — You can share a view-only version of your calendar with a partner or admin, useful for basic coordination.

**Reminders** — You can set reminders on individual events, and students can be invited to events so the lesson appears on their calendar.

**Cost** — Free.

**Integration** — Works with other Google services and many third-party apps.

## What Google Calendar Doesn't Do

**Online booking by students** — Google Calendar is not a booking system. Students cannot browse your availability and book a lesson directly. You still need to communicate the booking manually, agree a time, and add it yourself.

**Payment collection** — No payment functionality. You still need to arrange payment separately, whether by bank transfer, cash, or a separate payment link.

**Automated reminders** — You can manually set reminders, but sending SMS reminders to students automatically is not a native feature. Students don't get automatically notified 24 hours before their lesson from your calendar.

**Student records** — No place to store student contact details, lesson history, progress notes, or package balance. You'd need a separate system for this.

**Income tracking** — A calendar entry with a price note is not an income record. Reconciling your actual earnings requires a separate spreadsheet or accounting tool.

**AI phone answering** — Not a feature of any calendar tool.

**A public booking page** — Google Calendar can be published, but it's not a polished booking page with your photo, bio, pricing, and a payment form.

## The Real-World Gap

For a solo instructor taking 10 lessons a week, Google Calendar is manageable — the gaps can be filled with manual processes. For an instructor at 25+ lessons per week, the manual overhead of maintaining separate tools for scheduling, payment, records, reminders, and booking starts to consume significant time.

The question isn't whether Google Calendar is good. It's whether it's the right tool for a business operation that involves booking, payment, student management, and communication.

## Who Should Use Google Calendar

If you're teaching part-time, have very few students, and manage primarily through word-of-mouth with no need for online booking or payment, Google Calendar is fine. It costs nothing and does the basic scheduling job.
