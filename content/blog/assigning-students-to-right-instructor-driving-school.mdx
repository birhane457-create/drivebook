In a single-instructor business, matching is automatic — there's only one instructor. In a multi-instructor school, every new student is an assignment decision, and that decision has real consequences for retention, pass rates, and reviews.

A student who is matched poorly — wrong suburb, wrong vehicle type, wrong communication style — is more likely to leave, less likely to pass, and less likely to leave a good review.

## The Factors That Matter

**Location** — The most practical factor. An instructor who operates in Fremantle should not be assigned a student in Midland. Matching by service area reduces travel time, improves punctuality, and keeps both instructor and student from wasting time on logistics.

**Vehicle type** — A student who specifically needs to learn manual should be assigned an instructor with a manual car. This seems obvious, but in a busy school without a clear system, it gets missed.

**Language** — Perth has a large population of learner drivers who are more comfortable in languages other than English. An instructor who speaks Mandarin, Arabic, or Hindi has a significant advantage with certain students. Matching by language preference reduces anxiety and speeds up learning.

**Availability alignment** — A student who can only attend Saturday mornings should be assigned to an instructor with Saturday morning availability. Misaligned schedules cause lesson gaps and dropout.

**Instructor experience level** — Very nervous learners or those with particular challenges often benefit from a more experienced instructor. Newer instructors may be better suited to confident learners who just need supervised hours.

## The Cost of Poor Matching

A mismatch in location adds unnecessary travel time to every lesson. A mismatch in vehicle type means the student's licence won't cover what they actually want to drive. A mismatch in language means slower progress and more frustration.

Beyond the practical costs, a poorly matched student is more likely to switch instructors or leave the school entirely. In a market where word-of-mouth drives most new bookings, losing a student to a competitor — and having them tell their friends why — has a disproportionate cost.

## How to Match Systematically

Without a system, matching is ad hoc: whoever messages the WhatsApp group first, whoever replies to the admin first, whoever is the most convenient. This produces inconsistent results.

A systematic approach:

1. Collect student preferences upfront — suburb, vehicle type, language, availability
2. Filter available instructors by those criteria
3. Assign the best match; if multiple instructors qualify, assign to the one with most availability
4. Notify both the student and the instructor of the assignment

## What to Do When There's No Perfect Match

In a small school, you won't always have an instructor who ticks every box. In that case, prioritise in this order:

1. Location (non-negotiable — don't send an instructor 45 minutes for a lesson)
2. Vehicle type (if the student has a specific requirement)
3. Availability alignment
4. Language (if stated as a preference)

Communicate any limitations to the student upfront. "We have an instructor available in your area on Thursdays and Saturdays — does that work?" is much better than assigning someone unsuitable and hoping the student doesn't notice.

## Reassignment

Sometimes a match doesn't work out despite best efforts. A student requests a different instructor, or an instructor leaves the school. Having a clear reassignment process — who the student contacts, how quickly it happens, whether lessons are transferred — reduces the friction of these situations.
