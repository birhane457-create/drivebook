Roundabouts are disproportionately responsible for PDA failures in WA. The rules themselves aren't complicated — but many learners get to their test without having practised multi-lane roundabouts under real conditions, and the result shows.

This guide covers the rules, the common mistakes, and how to approach roundabouts so they feel automatic by test day.

## The Basic Rule

The fundamental rule of roundabouts in Australia: **give way to vehicles already in the roundabout**.

This applies regardless of which direction they're coming from. If a vehicle is in the roundabout and you're about to enter, you wait.

## Single-Lane Roundabouts

Single-lane roundabouts are straightforward once you understand the approach and exit:

**Approaching:**
- Reduce speed well before the roundabout — don't brake hard at the give-way line
- Look right first (vehicles come from the right in Australia)
- Wait for a safe gap in traffic or for the roundabout to be clear
- Enter when it's safe

**Indicating:**
- **Turning left:** Indicate left before entering
- **Going straight:** No indicator needed on approach; indicate left as you pass the exit before your turn
- **Turning right:** Indicate right before entering, then indicate left as you pass the exit before your turn
- **Full circle (U-turn):** Indicate right on approach, indicate left before your exit

**Exiting:**
- Indicate left before your exit and exit cleanly

## Multi-Lane Roundabouts

Multi-lane roundabouts are where most learners struggle. The key additional rules:

**Choosing your lane:**
- **Left turn:** Use the left lane
- **Straight ahead:** Use either lane (follow any road markings)
- **Right turn or full circle:** Use the right lane

**Staying in your lane:**
Once in the roundabout, stay in your lane. Do not drift or change lanes while inside the roundabout. This is one of the most common errors and is dangerous.

**Exiting from the right lane:**
If you entered the right lane and need to exit, signal left and exit when it's safe. You may need to cross the left lane — only do this when it's clear.

## The Mistakes That Fail PDA Candidates

**1. Not giving way to vehicles in the roundabout**
If you enter when a vehicle is already in the roundabout, that's a serious error. When in doubt, wait.

**2. Using the wrong lane**
Entering a multi-lane roundabout in the left lane when turning right is a common error. Study the lane markings before you enter.

**3. Not indicating when exiting**
You must indicate left before your exit, every time. Many learners forget this when concentrating on the roundabout itself.

**4. Incorrect lane on exit**
From the right lane, you should exit to the right lane of the road you're turning into, not the left.

**5. Speeding through a roundabout**
You must be able to stop if a vehicle enters. Slow down. Roundabouts are not through-speed zones.

## How to Practise

The only way roundabouts become automatic is repetition. Before your PDA:

1. Identify the roundabouts near your test centre — ask your instructor which ones are commonly included in PDA routes
2. Drive through each one multiple times in both directions
3. Specifically practise multi-lane roundabouts with your instructor calling out lane choices before you enter
4. Do at least one full mock test where roundabouts are assessed in real conditions

If you can handle the roundabouts near your test centre confidently, you've removed one of the most significant failure risks.
