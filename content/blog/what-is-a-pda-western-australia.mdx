If you're a learner driver in Western Australia, the PDA — Practical Driving Assessment — is the test that stands between you and your P plates. Understanding exactly what it involves, what assessors look for, and how to prepare will significantly improve your chances of passing first time.

## What Does PDA Stand For?

PDA stands for Practical Driving Assessment. It's the on-road driving test administered by the Department of Transport in Western Australia. Passing the PDA, along with your logbook hours and the Hazard Perception Test, is required to get your provisional (P) licence.

## Who Administers the PDA?

Your PDA is conducted by an approved assessor from the Department of Transport. The assessment takes approximately 30 to 45 minutes and is conducted on public roads near the test centre you book through.

Private driving instructors cannot administer the PDA themselves — you must book through DoT.

## What Are the Prerequisites?

Before you can take the PDA, you must:

- Have held your learner's permit for at least six months
- Have completed at least 50 hours of supervised driving, including at least 5 hours of night driving, as recorded in your logbook
- Have passed the Hazard Perception Test (HPT)
- Be at least 17 years old

All of these requirements must be met before booking your PDA.

## What Does the PDA Test?

The PDA assesses your ability to drive safely and competently on real roads. The assessor will give you directions and evaluate how well you handle:

**General driving skills:**
- Road positioning and lane management
- Speed management and following distance
- Observation — checking mirrors and blind spots
- Traffic light and sign compliance
- Giving way correctly at intersections

**Manoeuvres:**
- Reverse parallel parking
- Three-point turn (turn in the road)
- Uphill and downhill starts (where applicable to local roads)
- Reversing around a corner (not always included)

**Higher-speed driving:**
- Freeway or high-speed road driving is often included in metropolitan test routes

The exact route varies by test centre. Your local instructor will know the common routes used at centres near you.

## How Is the PDA Scored?

Assessors use a scoring system with two types of faults:

**Immediate failure items:** Certain actions result in an automatic fail regardless of how the rest of the test goes. These include:
- Causing or nearly causing an accident
- Disobeying a red light
- Driving dangerously
- Requiring the assessor to intervene (e.g. grab the wheel or apply the brake)

**Fault marks:** Minor errors are recorded as fault marks. A single fault mark won't fail you, but accumulating too many — or making the same error repeatedly — can result in a fail. The threshold is typically around 10 fault marks.

## What to Bring to Your PDA

- Your learner's permit (physical card, in date)
- Your logbook with all hours signed off
- Your Hazard Perception Test certificate
- The vehicle you'll use for the assessment (it must be roadworthy and have appropriate L plates)

If you're using your instructor's car, they'll handle the vehicle. If you're bringing a private vehicle, confirm with the test centre that it meets requirements.

## Booking Your PDA

PDAs are booked through the DoT website. Demand at popular test centres can mean wait times of several weeks. Book as soon as you've completed your logbook hours and HPT to avoid unnecessary delays.

You'll pay a fee at the time of booking. If you fail and need to rebook, you'll pay again.

## What Happens If You Fail?

If you don't pass, the assessor will give you a written report explaining the faults recorded. This is useful feedback for your next attempt.

There's no limit on how many times you can attempt the PDA. Review your assessment report carefully, work with your instructor on the specific areas that caused issues, and rebook when you're ready.

Most learners who fail the PDA cite nerves, observation errors, or lack of freeway practice as the main causes. These are all addressable with targeted preparation.

## How to Prepare

- Complete your 50+ logbook hours across a wide variety of road types and conditions
- Practise specifically on roads near your test centre
- Do your HPT as early as possible — it tests hazard awareness, not just theory
- Practise all manoeuvres until they're automatic
- Work with a professional driving instructor in the weeks leading up to your test

An experienced instructor who knows your local test routes is invaluable preparation. They've seen what assessors look for and can give you specific, targeted feedback.
