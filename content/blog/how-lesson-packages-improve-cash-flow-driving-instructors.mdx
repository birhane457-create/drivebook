Most driving instructors sell lessons one at a time. A student books, attends, pays. Then they may book again, or they may not. Income is unpredictable — some weeks are full, some weeks have gaps.

Lesson packages solve this by front-loading commitment and payment. Here's how they work and why they're worth considering.

## What a Lesson Package Is

A lesson package is a bundle of lessons sold at one time. Instead of paying per lesson, a student pays for five, ten, or fifteen lessons upfront and uses them over time.

For example:
- 6 lessons at a slight discount versus booking one at a time
- 10 lessons with a modest saving
- 15 lessons — typically used by students starting from scratch who need significant time to reach test-ready standard

The student gets a better rate. The instructor gets payment now, for future lessons.

## Why This Helps Cash Flow

When a student books one lesson, you receive that lesson's payment. When a student buys a 10-lesson package, you receive payment for ten lessons in a single transaction — before most of those lessons have occurred.

For an instructor whose lessons are $80 each, a 10-lesson package at a modest discount might be $720. That $720 arrives on the day of purchase, not spread across five weeks.

This changes the rhythm of your income. Instead of variable weekly income depending on how many students booked, you have recurring package purchases that provide larger, more predictable cash flows.

## Student Benefits

Students benefit too. A package is financially more efficient than booking lesson by lesson. Students who commit to a package also tend to progress faster — they're scheduling lessons more regularly rather than booking sporadically when they remember.

The psychological commitment of having pre-paid lessons motivates attendance. A student who's paid for ten lessons will make sure they show up for all ten.

## Lower Cancellation Rates

Students on packages cancel at lower rates than pay-per-lesson students. They've made a larger upfront commitment and are more invested in the process. A single lesson feels easier to skip. A package you've paid $700 for does not.

## How to Structure Packages

The most common structures are:

**Starter** — 5–6 lessons. Good for students who want to try before committing fully, or those who need refresher lessons.

**Standard** — 10 lessons. The most popular package size. Enough for significant progress and test preparation.

**Intensive** — 15+ lessons. Suitable for students starting from scratch who want to reach PDA-ready standard.

Pricing the packages at a modest saving (5–10%) is enough to incentivise purchase without significantly cutting into your margin.

## Packages and PDA Test Prep

A common add-on is a PDA Test Pack — a set of lessons specifically structured around test preparation, including practice on the route and covering all test elements. Pricing this separately makes the value clear and targets students who are close to test-ready.
