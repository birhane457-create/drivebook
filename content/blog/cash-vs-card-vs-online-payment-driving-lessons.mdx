Most driving instructors accept payment in one of three ways: cash after the lesson, bank transfer, or online payment at the time of booking. Each has genuine advantages and genuine downsides.

## Cash

**Advantages:**
- No payment processing fees
- Immediate — no waiting for transfers to clear
- Works for every student, including those without card payment capability
- Simple — no technology required

**Disadvantages:**
- Students often don't have the right amount and "round down"
- Some students "forget" and ask to pay next time — and then cancel
- No automatic record — you have to log it manually
- Doesn't scale: reconciling cash across 30+ lessons per week is tedious
- No audit trail if there's a dispute
- Can't accept payment in advance — lessons are always pay-after

The deeper problem with cash is that it structurally prevents advance payment, which means it does nothing to reduce cancellations or improve commitment.

## Bank Transfer

**Advantages:**
- Electronic record of every payment
- Students can pay in advance if asked
- No card processing fee on the instructor side

**Disadvantages:**
- Delays — transfers can take time to appear, especially over weekends
- Requires you to manually check your account before each lesson
- Awkward to chase students who haven't paid
- Different BSBs and account details get confused by students
- Doesn't integrate with your booking system — you have to manually reconcile who paid for what

Bank transfer is better than cash for record-keeping but still creates significant manual overhead.

## Online Payment at Booking

**Advantages:**
- Payment is confirmed before the lesson happens
- Every transaction is automatically recorded and categorised
- Integrates directly with your booking calendar — you can see exactly which bookings are paid
- Removes the need to chase payment entirely
- Students are more committed because they've paid upfront
- Packages can be purchased and automatically allocated to the student's account
- Income reports are generated automatically

**Disadvantages:**
- Payment processing fee (typically 1.5–3% via Stripe)
- Requires students to have a debit or credit card
- Not available for students who genuinely can't or won't pay by card

## What Most Instructors End Up Doing

The most practical approach for most instructors is to make online payment the default while keeping cash as an option for students who genuinely need it.

Default: students booking through your page pay at the time of booking via card. The lesson is confirmed, paid, and recorded automatically.

Exception: for students who prefer cash, the instructor records the booking manually and marks it as a cash payment. DriveBook's offline booking feature handles this — the booking appears in your calendar and income records even when payment is cash.

This approach captures the efficiency of online payment for the majority of students without turning away those who can't or won't pay by card.

## The Fee Question

The processing fee for online payment is real and worth acknowledging. At 1.5–2%, on an $80 lesson that's $1.20–$1.60. For many instructors, the time saved on manual reconciliation and the reduction in no-shows and cancellations more than compensates for this cost.

The comparison isn't "online payment fee vs no fee." It's "online payment fee vs the value of your time spent chasing payments and dealing with no-shows."
