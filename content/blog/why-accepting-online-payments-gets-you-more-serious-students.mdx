Asking for payment at the time of booking is not just about convenience. It's one of the most effective filters between serious students and those who will cancel without notice.

## The Psychology of Prepayment

When a student pays for a lesson in advance, they've made a financial commitment. Cancelling means losing that money (or navigating a refund). That friction is intentional — it's the same reason concert tickets are non-refundable and gym memberships are paid monthly rather than per visit.

Instructors who collect payment at booking consistently report fewer no-shows than those who collect cash on the day or send invoices after the lesson.

This isn't about being transactional. It's about aligning incentives. A student who's paid is motivated to show up and make the most of the lesson.

## Cash on the Day: What It Actually Costs

Cash collection seems simple. In practice:

- Students forget to bring the right amount
- Some "forgot" and ask to pay next time, then cancel
- You have to track who's paid and who hasn't
- Reconciling your actual income against your teaching hours takes time
- There's no automatic record if there's a dispute later

For a solo instructor taking 25 lessons per week, this administrative load adds up. A system where every booking is paid before it happens eliminates most of it.

## Bank Transfer: Slightly Better, Still Slow

Some instructors use bank transfer as a middle ground. It's better than cash but still has problems:

- Students often don't transfer until the last minute (or after)
- You need to manually check your account before each lesson
- Transfers can be delayed
- Following up on unpaid bookings is uncomfortable

## Online Payment at Booking: What Changes

When students pay through a booking page at the time of booking:

**You know every lesson on your calendar is paid.** No chasing, no checking, no awkward conversations.

**Cancellations come with a decision.** Students who need to cancel have to actively request a refund rather than simply not showing up. This alone changes behaviour.

**Your income is predictable.** You can look at your week and know what you'll earn before it starts.

**The admin disappears.** No cash to count, no bank transfers to reconcile, no spreadsheet tracking who paid what.

## What About Students Who Prefer Cash?

Some students — particularly older adults or international students — genuinely prefer cash. You don't have to eliminate cash entirely. DriveBook allows you to record offline (cash) bookings in your dashboard, so even cash transactions appear in your schedule and income records.

The point isn't to refuse cash — it's to make online payment the default path, so the majority of your bookings arrive already paid.

## Packages: Even Better for Cash Flow

Lesson packages — where a student pays for six or ten lessons upfront — improve cash flow further. You receive payment for multiple lessons in one transaction. The student commits to a longer engagement, which means better retention and fewer weeks where you're partially booked.

DriveBook's package system lets you create custom lesson bundles. Students can purchase them directly from your booking page.

## The Student's Experience

It's worth considering this from the student's side too. Paying online before a lesson is:

- Fast (takes 2 minutes)
- Familiar (they already buy everything else online)
- Reassuring (they get an immediate confirmation)

Students who pay online typically show up with more confidence because the transaction is settled before they arrive. There's no awkward money moment at the start or end of a lesson.
