Test day nerves are normal. One of the things that makes them worse is not knowing what to expect when you arrive at the test centre. The unknown creates anxiety even for learner drivers who are technically well-prepared.

Here's exactly what happens from the moment you arrive at a WA Department of Transport test centre for your Practical Driving Assessment.

## Before You Arrive

Allow yourself extra time. Arriving rushed to a driving test is a poor start. Plan to be at the test centre 10–15 minutes before your appointment time.

Double-check you have everything:
- Your learner's permit (current, not expired)
- Your logbook (completed hours, all entries signed)
- Hazard Perception Test certificate
- The vehicle you'll be using (if not using an instructor's car)

If you're using a private vehicle, confirm it has current L plates fitted front and rear. The vehicle will be checked.

## Arriving at the Test Centre

Most test centres in metropolitan Perth have a designated waiting area. Tell the reception staff you're there for your PDA and provide your name and appointment time. You'll be asked to sit and wait for your assessor.

Use this time to focus. Some learners find it helpful to do a quiet breathing exercise rather than sitting anxiously. Avoid scrolling through social media or engaging in conversations that raise your stress level.

## The Vehicle Check

Before you go out on the road, the assessor will do a brief vehicle check. They'll confirm the vehicle is roadworthy and properly equipped with:
- Working lights (headlights, brake lights, indicators)
- Horn
- Mirrors (driver side, passenger side, and rear-view)
- Seatbelts
- L plates (front and rear, clearly visible)

If anything fails the vehicle check, the assessment cannot proceed. This is rare for properly maintained vehicles, but worth confirming in advance.

## Meeting Your Assessor

Your assessor will introduce themselves and briefly explain the structure of the assessment. They're not there to intimidate you — their job is to objectively assess your driving competence. They've done this hundreds of times and they're used to learner drivers being nervous.

You'll be asked to sign a form confirming your details and acknowledging the terms of the assessment. Read it briefly and sign.

## In the Vehicle

Before starting the engine, the assessor will ask you to adjust your seat and mirrors if needed, and confirm you're comfortable. They may ask you to identify a control or two — windscreen wipers, hazard lights — as part of the initial check.

Then you drive. The assessor will give you directions turn by turn. You don't need to know where you're going — just follow the directions given.

**Don't narrate your actions** unless you've been specifically trained to do so. A natural, quiet driver is assessed the same as one who talks through every decision.

## If You Make a Mistake

Most learners make at least one minor error during their PDA. One or two fault marks will not fail you. The assessor is looking at your overall competence, not whether you're perfect.

If you make a mistake:
- Don't panic
- Don't apologise excessively or comment on it
- Focus on the next instruction
- Continue driving calmly

Assessors note how you recover from errors. Continuing to drive well after a mistake shows composure and control.

## After the Assessment

When you return to the test centre, the assessor will tell you straight away whether you've passed or need to come back.

**If you pass:** You'll complete paperwork to begin the process of getting your provisional licence. Congratulations — your driving journey moves to the next stage.

**If you don't pass:** The assessor will give you a written report listing the faults recorded. Take this seriously — it's specific, useful feedback. Review it with your instructor, work on the areas identified, and rebook when you're confident those issues are resolved.

Don't rebook too quickly. A week or two of targeted practice on the specific failure points is more useful than rebooking the next available slot without addressing the underlying issues.
