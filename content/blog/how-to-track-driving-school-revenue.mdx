Many driving school owners understand their business intuitively — they know roughly how much comes in and roughly how much goes out. But "roughly" isn't enough when it comes to tax time, scaling decisions, or understanding whether each instructor is actually profitable.

You don't need an accountant to get basic financial visibility. You do need a system.

## What You Actually Need to Track

For a driving school, the core financial picture comes down to four numbers:

**Gross revenue** — Total lesson income before any deductions. Every completed booking multiplied by its price.

**Instructor payouts** — What you pay out to your instructors. For a school with employed or contracted instructors, this is your largest cost.

**Platform and payment fees** — What you pay to booking platforms, payment processors, and any other per-transaction costs.

**Net revenue** — What's left after payouts and fees. This is what you actually keep.

Everything else — vehicle costs, insurance, admin tools — layers on top of these four numbers.

## The Problem With Manual Tracking

If you're tracking income in a spreadsheet fed by manual entries, you're working harder than you need to and introducing error risk. Manual entry means:
- You forget to log a booking
- A cash lesson never makes it into the spreadsheet
- Refunds and cancellations get missed
- You reconcile at the end of the month and can't explain a discrepancy

The solution is to route as much income as possible through a system that logs it automatically.

## Handling Cash and Offline Bookings

Not every lesson goes through the online system. A student who pays cash, a lesson booked by phone, an offline arrangement — these need to be recorded too, or your financial picture is incomplete.

DriveBook lets you record offline bookings manually (available on PRO and above) so they appear in your records alongside online bookings. This keeps your data complete even when payment doesn't go through the platform.

## Weekly and Monthly Reviews

Get into the habit of a brief financial review:

**Weekly (5 minutes):** Check total lessons completed, total revenue, any outstanding payments or unresolved issues.

**Monthly (20 minutes):** Review total revenue vs the previous month, check instructor payout accuracy, identify any anomalies (unusually high cancellations, a student with an overdue balance, a month that's lower than expected).

This doesn't require financial expertise. It requires consistency. Most issues that turn into serious problems were visible weeks or months earlier to someone who was looking.

## When to Bring in an Accountant

The DIY approach works well for day-to-day visibility. But there are points where professional advice is worth the cost:

- Setting up your business structure (sole trader vs company)
- First tax return as a driving school
- When you take on employees (PAYG, superannuation, payroll tax)
- When revenue grows significantly and you need to make investment decisions

A good accountant doesn't just do your tax return — they find deductions you'd miss and structure your affairs more efficiently. For a growing driving school, this often pays for itself.
