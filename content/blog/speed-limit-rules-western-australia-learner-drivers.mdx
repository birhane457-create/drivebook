Speed is one of the most important factors in road safety, and understanding speed limits is one of the first things a learner driver in Western Australia needs to master. It's also one of the most common sources of faults in the Practical Driving Assessment — not from driving too fast, but from not managing speed consistently and appropriately.

## Default Speed Limits in WA

Western Australia uses a set of default speed limits that apply unless a sign indicates otherwise:

**50 km/h** — Built-up areas (urban streets where there are houses, shops, or other buildings along both sides of the road)

**100 km/h** — Outside built-up areas (most rural roads and highways without specific signage)

**110 km/h** — Freeways and some divided highways (where signed)

These defaults only apply when there is no speed limit sign. If a sign is present, the sign applies.

## School Zones

School zones in WA operate at **40 km/h** during school hours on school days. The zone applies when children are arriving or departing — typically 7:30am–9:00am and 2:30pm–4:00pm.

Speed cameras actively enforce school zones in WA. For learner drivers, it's important to recognise school zone signage and reduce speed early — not just at the sign itself.

## Shared Zones

Shared zones, where vehicles and pedestrians share the same space, have a default limit of **10 km/h**. These are found near pedestrian areas and some shopping precincts.

## How Speed Limit Signs Work

Speed limit signs are white circles with a red border and black numbers. When you pass one, the limit it shows applies immediately and continues until you see another speed limit sign or leave the zone.

A few important points:
- When you enter a built-up area (crossing the boundary), the 50 km/h default applies even if there's no sign
- Speed advisory signs (yellow diamonds with a number) are not legal limits — they're recommendations for a specific hazard like a curve
- Variable speed limit signs (digital signs on freeways) are legally enforceable

## Managing Speed During Your PDA

Speed management during the PDA isn't just about not exceeding the limit. Assessors also note:

**Travelling significantly below the limit.** Driving at 40 km/h in a 60 km/h zone when there's no hazard is assessed as poor speed management.

**Inconsistent speed.** Accelerating and braking repeatedly rather than maintaining a smooth, consistent speed shows poor control.

**Late speed reduction.** Slowing down at the sign rather than approaching at the right speed shows poor forward planning.

**Failure to return to limit after a hazard.** If you slow for a parked car and then don't accelerate back to the limit, assessors notice.

The goal is appropriate, smooth, consistent speed — not the maximum possible, but not unnecessarily slow either.

## School Zone Timing

A common mistake for learner drivers is confusing when school zones apply. In WA, school zones are active:

- On school days only
- During the designated times shown on the signs (usually marked with flashing lights or time plates)

Outside school hours — and on weekends and school holidays — the standard speed limit applies through school zones. The signs will indicate when the reduced limit is active.

If you're not certain whether a school zone is active, err on the side of 40 km/h. It's better to be cautious than to speed past a school unknowingly.

## GPS and Speed Displays

Many learner drivers use GPS navigation apps that display the current speed limit on screen. This can be a useful reference, but GPS speed limit data isn't always current — particularly in areas with recent roadworks or newly changed limits.

Always treat posted signs as authoritative. The GPS is a guide, not a substitute for reading the road.

## Speed and Following Distance

Speed management isn't just about the limit — it's about the relationship between your speed and your ability to stop safely. At 60 km/h, your stopping distance in good conditions is roughly 45 metres. At 80 km/h, it's approximately 75 metres.

As your speed increases, leave more following distance. Use the three-second rule as a minimum: the car in front passes a point, you reach the same point three seconds later. Extend this in wet conditions, at night, or when following a heavy vehicle.
