Parking seems like the least complicated part of driving until you get a fine or block someone's driveway. Western Australia's parking rules are specific and enforced — understanding them before you have your licence means you won't be learning by error after you do.

## No Parking vs No Stopping

These two signs are often confused:

**No Stopping** (yellow sign with a crossed circle): You cannot stop the vehicle at all in this zone — even briefly, even to drop someone off. The moment the vehicle stops, you're in violation.

**No Parking** (black text on white): You cannot park here, but you may stop briefly (up to two minutes) to drop off or pick up passengers. The driver must stay in the vehicle.

Both signs apply 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, unless a time plate below the sign specifies otherwise.

## Time-Limited Parking

Signs like "2P" or "1/2P" mean you can park for that maximum time — 2 hours, or 30 minutes. The time limit begins from when you park, and you must leave for a minimum period before returning to the same zone.

**You cannot reset your time by moving the car slightly and coming back.** Parking officers note tyre positions and can detect this.

Time limits are active during the hours shown on the accompanying time plate (e.g., "Mon–Fri 8am–6pm"). Outside those hours, parking is generally unrestricted unless another sign applies.

## Clearways

Clearways are sections of road where parking is prohibited during specific peak hours to keep traffic flowing. They're marked with red and yellow signs and commonly apply:
- Weekday mornings and afternoons on arterial roads
- Near schools during pickup and drop-off times

Outside clearway hours, parking may be permitted (unless another sign overrides). During clearway hours, vehicles will be ticketed and often towed.

## Where You Cannot Park — Always

Regardless of signs, you cannot park:

- Within 10 metres of an intersection (unless a sign permits it)
- On a footpath or nature strip (unless signed)
- In front of a driveway or access point
- On a painted island or median strip
- In a disabled bay without a current disabled permit displayed
- In a no-parking or no-stopping zone
- On a zebra crossing or pedestrian crossing
- Within 20 metres of an emergency stop zone or bus zone when it's active
- On a bridge, in a tunnel, or at a crest (where you'd be concealed from other drivers)

## Hills: Kerbing Your Wheels

When parking on a hill, you must turn your wheels to prevent the vehicle rolling if the handbrake fails:

**Facing downhill:** Turn wheels to the right (toward the kerb). If the vehicle moves, the kerb stops it.

**Facing uphill:** Turn wheels to the left (away from the kerb). If the vehicle moves, it turns into the kerb.

On a level surface, wheels should be straight.

This is tested in the PDA and enforced in real life — you can be fined for not kerbing your wheels on a gradient.

## Parallel Parking in Practice

When parallel parking on a public road:
- Park within 30 cm of the kerb
- Park in the direction of traffic (don't park on the wrong side of the road facing oncoming traffic)
- Don't block driveways or fire hydrants

The technique for parallel parking is covered in more detail in our [reversing guide](/blog/how-to-teach-reversing-learner-drivers), but the rules are worth knowing independently.

## Loading Zones

A loading zone (blue "L" sign) is for commercial loading and unloading only. Private vehicles cannot park in a loading zone, even briefly. Passenger drop-offs are not loading.

Some loading zones are timed (e.g., "Loading Zone — 15 min") and permit very brief stops for dropping off goods. Check the sign.

## Parking Inspectors and Enforcement

WA councils actively enforce parking. Officers use electronic devices to log tyre positions and time. Common places with active enforcement:
- Hospital precincts
- Shopping centre surrounds
- Train station carparks
- CBD streets
- School zones

Fines for parking violations are not minor. Learn the rules properly and avoid them.

## How to Read a Parking Sign

Many parking signs have multiple components stacked together. Read them from top to bottom:

1. The rule (e.g., 2P, No Parking)
2. The days it applies (e.g., Mon–Fri, Mon–Sat)
3. The hours it applies (e.g., 8am–6pm)
4. Any exceptions

If a sign has no time plate, the rule applies 24/7. If there are multiple signs, each applies within its specified hours.
