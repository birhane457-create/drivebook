The three-point turn — also called a turn in the road — is a core manoeuvre for learner drivers and is commonly tested in the Practical Driving Assessment. Many learners find it intimidating at first, but with a clear understanding of each step, it becomes one of the more manageable skills to master.

## What Is a Three-Point Turn?

A three-point turn is used to reverse the direction of your vehicle on a narrow road where a U-turn isn't possible. Instead of turning in one movement, you complete three separate moves to turn the car around.

The name comes from three movements:
1. Forward and across the road
2. Reverse and toward the kerb behind you
3. Forward into the new direction

## When to Use It

You'd use a three-point turn when you need to reverse direction on a two-lane road and there isn't enough room for a U-turn. Your assessor may ask you to perform one during your PDA, typically on a quiet residential street.

## Before You Start

Choose a safe, clear stretch of road. Check for:
- Oncoming traffic in both directions
- Pedestrians, cyclists, and driveways
- Sufficient visibility ahead and behind

Signal your intention to pull over if you need to slow down and prepare. Apply the handbrake when stationary.

## Step-by-Step

**Step 1: Start from the left side of the road**

Signal right if other traffic is present. Check mirrors and blind spots. Release the handbrake, and steer hard right as you move slowly forward toward the opposite kerb. Your aim is to cross the road at an angle, not drive straight across it.

Move slowly — crawl pace — so you have time to steer and stop. Stop before you reach the opposite kerb. Apply the handbrake.

**Step 2: Reverse**

Select reverse gear. Check all mirrors and over both shoulders before moving. Steer hard left as you reverse slowly back toward the kerb behind you. Look mainly through the rear window but glance forward regularly — especially toward the kerb you were nearest in Step 1.

Stop before reaching the kerb behind you. Apply the handbrake.

**Step 3: Drive forward in the new direction**

Select first gear. Steer to straighten the car in the new direction of travel. Check for traffic, release the handbrake, and move off. Signal if appropriate for the road.

## Common Mistakes

**Moving too fast.** Speed is the most common error. You need low speed to control your steering and stop before hitting a kerb.

**Not checking blind spots.** Before every movement — forward or reverse — check mirrors and blind spots. Failing to observe is a common PDA failure point.

**Hitting the kerb.** This will likely result in a fault on your PDA. Move slowly enough that you have time to stop before the kerb.

**Wide angle on the first move.** If you don't steer early and sharply enough on the first move, you won't get far enough across the road and the second move becomes very difficult. Commit to the full steering lock early.

**Rushing.** Three-point turns take a little time. Assessors aren't timing you. Take the time to check observations at each stage.

## How Many Points Can a Three-Point Take?

Ideally two or three movements. If you need a fourth or fifth move, that's called a five-point turn, and while it's not automatically a failure, it does indicate the manoeuvre wasn't executed as efficiently as it should be. In most PDA settings, additional moves won't fail you but will be noted.

## Practise on Quiet Streets

Practice the three-point turn on a quiet street with good visibility, not outside a school or near an intersection. Your instructor will guide you to an appropriate location. Practise until the movement feels natural and your observations are automatic before your PDA.
