The Hazard Perception Test (HPT) is a computer-based test that every Western Australian learner driver must pass before booking their Practical Driving Assessment. Understanding how it works — and how to prepare effectively — makes a significant difference to your result.

## What Is the Hazard Perception Test?

The HPT is a video-based test administered at Department of Transport test centres in WA. It shows you video clips filmed from a driver's perspective on real WA roads. As you watch, you identify developing hazards — situations that could require the driver to react, such as a pedestrian stepping off a kerb, a car pulling out from a side street, or an intersection that requires slowing.

The test measures how quickly and accurately you identify hazards. It's testing genuine hazard perception skill, not road rules knowledge.

## What Counts as a Hazard?

The HPT distinguishes between:

**Developing hazards:** A hazard that is about to require action — a pedestrian approaching the road, a vehicle beginning to turn across your path. These are what you're clicking for.

**Potential hazards:** Situations that could become hazards but haven't yet — a parked car on the left, a cyclist in the distance. You note these but they don't trigger a click.

The test rewards early, accurate identification of developing hazards. The faster you click after the hazard starts to develop (but before it fully develops), the higher your score.

## How the Scoring Works

Each hazard clip has a scoring window. Clicking early in the window scores more points than clicking late in the window. Clicking before the hazard develops (anticipating too early) scores nothing.

The test is designed so that random clicking scores poorly. You need genuine hazard awareness — the skill of scanning the scene and identifying what's about to require attention.

A passing score in WA is 9 out of 14 or higher on most versions of the test.

## How to Prepare

**Practice with official materials.** The WA Department of Transport provides practice HPT materials. These are the most relevant preparation because they use the same format, scoring system, and types of hazard scenarios as the real test. Don't rely entirely on overseas YouTube videos — WA road environments, signage, and hazard types are specific.

**Practise active scanning.** One of the skills the HPT tests is visual scanning — regularly checking mirrors, looking ahead, checking side roads. In your driving lessons, practise this actively. Ask your instructor to give you feedback on your scanning behaviour.

**Watch the whole scene.** A common mistake in HPT practice is focusing on the centre of the screen. Real hazards develop from the sides, from behind parked cars, from pedestrians on footpaths approaching the road. Train yourself to scan the full frame, not just the middle.

**Don't click randomly.** Rapid, random clicking is detected by the test and penalised. Click deliberately when you identify a specific developing hazard.

**Practise timing.** The scoring window is forgiving of being a second or two early or late within the window. What kills your score is clicking after the hazard has fully materialised (too late) or clicking repeatedly for the same hazard.

## On the Day

The HPT is computer-based and conducted in the test centre. You'll watch the video clips on a screen and click the mouse when you identify a developing hazard.

The test takes approximately 45 minutes. You can't pause or go back to a clip.

Read the instructions on screen before the test begins. The interface is straightforward but it's worth confirming you understand how to register a response before the scored clips start.

## When to Take the HPT

Don't leave the HPT until the week before your PDA. Take it as soon as you're eligible and your hazard awareness skills are developing — typically after you've had several months of driving lessons.

Passing the HPT early removes it as a bottleneck and lets you focus your preparation on the PDA itself. If you need to retake it, you'll have time.

## If You Don't Pass First Time

You can retake the HPT. There's a waiting period between attempts. Use the time to review the feedback from your attempt, continue your driving lessons with a focus on hazard scanning, and practise with the official materials again before rebooking.

Most learners who fail the HPT on their first attempt pass on a subsequent attempt after targeted preparation.
